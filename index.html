Pre-Pilot Preparation

The pre-pilot phase prepares the division for a controlled, measurable, low-risk introduction of F.R.I.E.N.D. before store-level deployment.

1. Strategic Alignment

Purpose

Establish clear alignment between division leadership, district leadership, store leadership, department leaders, and frontline associates.

Key Actions

  • Define the business problem F.R.I.E.N.D. is solving.
  • Confirm pilot goals and measurable success standards.
  • Identify three pilot stores: low, medium, and high volume.
  • Clarify executive sponsorship and decision-making authority.
  • Build a communication plan for store and district leadership.

Expected Outcome

Leadership enters the pilot with shared expectations, clear accountability, and a measurable definition of success.

2. Store Selection

Recommended Pilot Model

  • Low-volume store: Tests simplicity, adoption speed, and basic labor efficiency.
  • Medium-volume store: Tests balanced operational complexity and daily execution support.
  • High-volume store: Tests scalability, pressure points, labor intensity, and department workload.

Selection Criteria

  • Store leadership willing to participate actively.
  • Departments with measurable pain points.
  • Existing TC-52 or mobile-device usage.
  • Strong enough operational discipline to collect reliable data.
  • Enough variation in sales volume, staffing, and workload to test adaptability.
3. Process Mapping

Processes to Map Before Pilot

  • Opening and closing routines.
  • Department task lists.
  • Production planning.
  • Freshness and shrink tracking.
  • Thaw-and-sell execution.
  • Food safety checks.
  • Safety walks.
  • Associate training steps.
  • Manager follow-up routines.
  • Store leader and district leader review cadence.

Reason

F.R.I.E.N.D. should not simply digitize existing chaos. The pre-pilot phase should identify what is working, what is inconsistent, and what should be standardized before technology is layered on top of the operation.

4. Data and Metrics Setup

Baseline Data Needed

  • Sales by department.
  • Labor hours by department.
  • Shrink and markdown trends.
  • Out-of-stock patterns.
  • Training completion rates.
  • Task completion rates.
  • Food safety compliance records.
  • Customer service indicators.
  • Associate retention and turnover indicators.

Why This Matters

Without a baseline, the pilot cannot prove impact. Pre-pilot measurement allows leadership to compare before-and-after results with greater confidence.

Pilot Program

The pilot program validates F.R.I.E.N.D. in real-world retail conditions across three store formats before broader deployment.

Month 1 — Implementation

Install pilot environment, configure dashboards, train store leaders, department leaders, and associates.

Month 2 — Adoption

Daily use begins. Departments start utilizing task lists, training modules, production guidance, and leadership dashboards.

Month 3 — Measurement

Collect labor, shrink, sales, compliance, training, and execution data.

Month 4-6 — Optimization

Refine workflows, adjust dashboards, gather leadership feedback, and validate ROI assumptions.

Regional Rollout

Following a successful pilot, the platform expands to a regional deployment of approximately 20-30 stores.

Phase 1 — Regional Leadership Alignment

District leaders and store leaders receive training and establish regional performance expectations.

Phase 2 — Store Expansion

Roll out F.R.I.E.N.D. to additional stores while preserving lessons learned during pilot implementation.

Phase 3 — Standardization

Standardize dashboards, execution metrics, training pathways, and accountability systems across the region.

Phase 4 — Continuous Improvement

Regional leaders leverage data to identify opportunities, replicate best practices, and accelerate performance.
