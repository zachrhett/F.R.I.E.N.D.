Executive Summary

Purpose of F.R.I.E.N.D.

F.R.I.E.N.D. is designed to act as a division-level operational intelligence layer. It supports store teams by centralizing task visibility, training guidance, production execution, food safety, shrink awareness, schedule alignment, and leadership follow-up.

Primary Business Focus
  • Improve task execution consistency.
  • Reduce shrink through better visibility and accountability.
  • Support new associate training and onboarding.
  • Improve labor productivity through clearer direction.
  • Give store, district, and division leaders real-time operational insight.

1. Pre-Pilot

The pre-pilot phase prepares the division for a controlled test before broader investment.

Detailed Pre-Pilot Breakdown
  • Identify pilot stores: low-volume, medium-volume, and high-volume.
  • Map workflows by department.
  • Identify current execution gaps.
  • Build leadership dashboard requirements.
  • Establish success metrics before launch.
  • Prepare TC-52 or mobile-device workflow structure.

2. Pre-Pilot Cost

Initial costs may include planning, leadership alignment, technology preparation, training materials, workflow mapping, and technical setup.

Cost Categories
  • Device preparation and testing.
  • Software mockup and prototype development.
  • Training material creation.
  • Leadership alignment meetings.
  • Data collection process design.
  • Support labor for pilot launch.

3. Pilot Program

The pilot should be tested across three stores: one low-volume, one medium-volume, and one high-volume location.

3–6 Month Pilot Structure
  • Month 1: Setup, training, baseline data collection.
  • Months 2–3: Active use and leadership feedback.
  • Months 4–5: Adjustments, department refinement, deeper adoption.
  • Month 6: Results review and rollout recommendation.

4. Regional Rollout

After pilot results are reviewed, F.R.I.E.N.D. can expand to 20–30 stores with regional leadership support.

Regional Scaling Plan
  • Deploy to selected stores based on operational readiness.
  • Train store leaders and department leaders.
  • Use pilot stores as reference models.
  • Track adoption, execution, shrink, and training completion.

5. Division Rollout

A full division rollout would scale the program across all 155 King Soopers stores with structured training, support, and performance tracking.

Division Rollout Requirements
  • Standardized store launch playbook.
  • District-level support structure.
  • Division dashboard for operational visibility.
  • Training compliance tracking.
  • Ongoing feedback and refinement loop.

6. ROI

ROI should be measured through shrink reduction, labor productivity, training completion, execution consistency, and manager time savings.

ROI Measurement Areas
  • Reduced shrink from stronger task visibility.
  • Reduced missed production opportunities.
  • Improved labor deployment.
  • Faster new associate onboarding.
  • Improved compliance with food safety and operational standards.

7. Cost of Inaction

Inaction may result in continued shrink, inconsistent training, missed sales opportunities, poor communication, unnecessary labor waste, and leadership blind spots.

Hidden Costs of Inaction
  • Lost productivity from unclear priorities.
  • Inconsistent execution across stores.
  • Training gaps for new associates.
  • Shrink caused by missed process controls.
  • Leadership time lost searching for operational clarity.

8. Red Team

A responsible rollout must also examine possible negative impacts, adoption risks, training resistance, cost overruns, and implementation challenges.

Potential Risks
  • Store teams may resist another system if value is not clear.
  • Too many alerts could create fatigue.
  • Bad data could lead to poor decisions.
  • Training time may temporarily affect productivity.
  • Technology must support workers, not overwhelm them.

9. 10 Point Rollout

The rollout should be structured, measurable, and adaptable.

10 Point Rollout Plan
  • 1. Define success metrics.
  • 2. Select pilot stores.
  • 3. Map department workflows.
  • 4. Build prototype dashboard.
  • 5. Train pilot leaders.
  • 6. Launch 3-store pilot.
  • 7. Collect data and feedback.
  • 8. Adjust system design.
  • 9. Expand to 20–30 stores.
  • 10. Scale across the division.

Executive Closing Statement

The D2 Developers are proud to introduce F.R.I.E.N.D. — a practical, scalable, and people-centered operational intelligence system designed to help store teams succeed while giving leadership clearer visibility into execution across the King Soopers Division.
