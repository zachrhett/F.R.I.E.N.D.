Executive Summary

F.R.I.E.N.D. is a proposed integrated retail execution platform designed to connect store leadership, department leaders, and associates through one unified operational system. The goal is simple: make work clearer, faster, more measurable, and easier to execute across the King Soopers division.

Proposal Sections

1. Pre-Pilot

  • Define scope
  • Select pilot stores
  • Map operational gaps
The pre-pilot phase prepares the organization before launch. This includes choosing low, medium, and high-volume stores, identifying current pain points, defining success metrics, and aligning store and division leadership.

2. Pre-Pilot Cost

  • Device readiness
  • Training labor
  • System setup
Cost planning should include technology review, Zebra/TC-52 readiness, training hours, leadership preparation, reporting setup, and support during implementation.

3. Pilot Program

  • 3 stores
  • 3–6 months
  • Data collection
The pilot tests F.R.I.E.N.D. across three store environments: low volume, medium volume, and high volume. The pilot measures execution, adoption, labor impact, shrink impact, and leadership usefulness.

4. Regional Rollout

  • 20–30 stores
  • Scale support
  • Regional learning
After the pilot, the program expands regionally. This stage validates whether the system works across multiple store types and leadership styles before division-wide adoption.

5. Division Rollout

  • All-store deployment
  • Training cadence
  • Leadership dashboard
The division rollout scales the system across the King Soopers division using lessons from the pilot and regional rollout. The goal is consistent execution, measurable accountability, and improved visibility.

6. ROI Analysis

  • Labor efficiency
  • Shrink reduction
  • Execution consistency
ROI should be measured through labor optimization, shrink reduction, faster training, improved task completion, stronger compliance, and better department execution.

Cost of Inaction

The hidden cost of inaction is continued fragmentation: disconnected communication, inconsistent task execution, training gaps, missed opportunities, preventable shrink, and leadership blind spots across stores and departments.

Red Team Review

A successful rollout must address risk early: adoption resistance, unclear ownership, technology friction, training fatigue, and over-complication. F.R.I.E.N.D. should be tested, challenged, simplified, and improved before full division rollout.
